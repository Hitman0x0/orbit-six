The five orbits

One lap of the sun for every year, from 9 September 2021 to today. The first two were only screens. Then Jakarta, and everything after it.

  1. Orbit I2021–22

    The year of the screen

    Where it began: 9 September 2021. Two time zones and one conversation that never really ended. Learning an entire person through a screen, which everyone says is impossible, and isn't.

  2. Orbit II2022–23

    The year we learned to wait

    Good morning arriving in your evening. Calls that outlasted the battery. Somewhere in here the distance stopped being a problem to solve and became a thing we were simply good at.

  3. Orbit III2023–24

    Jakarta

    22 March 2024. Nine hundred and twenty-five days of waiting, then an arrivals hall, and then no distance at all. Everything before it was a promise. This was the proof.

  4. Orbit IV2024–25

    The year I met your family

    2 January 2025, and a second flight. Mama, Dad, Cece — and the very particular terror of wanting badly to be liked. Being let into the room you grew up in is its own kind of arrival: you stop being someone she talks to and become someone they know.

  5. Orbit V2025–26

    Today — and the lap where we close it

    The fullest lap yet: Cece in November, then Bandung in June, kept quiet from everyone. Five orbits finished from opposite points of the same spinning planet, and four flights that took the distance to nothing. Orbit six is the one where the map stops mattering.

Joya —

On the ninth of September, 2021, I had no idea that a conversation with someone an ocean away was going to become the fixed point everything else turned around. I'm glad I didn't know. I got to find out slowly, one orbit at a time, which is the best way to be told good news.

Five years measured in hours of difference and kilometres I couldn't shorten. You were worth every one of them — the time zones, the airports, the bad connections, the waiting. You always were.

And then Jakarta, 22 March 2024, when the number finally went to zero. I would do all 925 days again for that one. Then January, when you walked me into your family and they let me stay. Then November, and a whole trip with nothing in it but you and Cece. Then Bandung, which we told absolutely nobody about. Four flights, and every one of them worth the airport.

Here's to orbit six, and the lap where we stop counting kilometres.

— Roze, 9 September 2026
